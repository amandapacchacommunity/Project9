Risk Overview Targeting Relationships

Relationships

This page explains how coded donor, household, and foundation records connect to one another so that multiple specialists can read the portfolio the same way.

Audience: Advancement + Operations
Executive summary Relationship records provide structural context, not financial totals. Their value is in supporting coordination, avoiding duplicated outreach, and clarifying linked entities.

What this page is for

  • Clarify who is linked to whom
  • Support household and foundation-aware outreach
  • Reduce conflicting or duplicated solicitations
  • Give specialists a shared coding system

How to interpret totals

  • Relationship records = structural coded entries
  • Household-linked count = records tied to a household code
  • Foundation-linked count = records tied to a foundation
  • These are structural totals, not giving totals

Key takeaways

Finding What it means Recommended action
Some records carry both household and foundation context More than one specialist may need aligned outreach Coordinate strategy before major solicitation
Foundations follow different timing than individuals Institutional work often requires longer lead time Separate proposal planning from personal stewardship
Household coding reduces fragmented outreach Multiple records may represent one broader relationship context Review linked records together before contact

Decision guide

  • If a record is foundation-linked, separate institutional timing from personal timing
  • If last touch is stale, reassess relationship coordination first

Method and limitations

  • All examples are synthetic and coded for public use
  • Relationship pages show structure, not financial outcomes
  • Linked counts may overlap across donor, household, and foundation views
  • One real-world relationship can appear in multiple coded forms
